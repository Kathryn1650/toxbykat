Myths

No, you won't look frozen. Here's why that fear exists.

By Kat, RN

"Frozen face" is the single biggest reason people put off trying Botox — and it's almost always based on seeing someone who was overdosed, not on Botox itself doing something inherently unnatural. Let's break down where that fear actually comes from.

Myth

Botox always makes your face stop moving.

In reality, the "stuck" look comes almost entirely from dosing — either too many units in one area, or product placed somewhere it restricts movement you actually want to keep, like your smile or the top of your cheeks. A conservative, well-mapped treatment relaxes specific lines while leaving your natural expression intact.

Myth

Once you start, you have to keep going or your face gets worse.

Botox is fully temporary — it wears off gradually over three to four months, and your muscles return to their normal movement. There's no dependency effect and no rebound where lines come back worse than before. If you decide it's not for you, you simply stop, and things go back to how they were.

Myth

You have to go big to see results.

Some of the best results I've done are also the most boring to talk about — nobody notices anything changed, they just say you look rested. That's usually a sign it was dosed well, not that it was underdosed.

How I avoid it

I start conservative every time, especially with new clients. It's much easier to add a touch-up in two weeks than to undo something that's already in your system. I also spend real time watching how your face moves naturally before I ever pick up a needle — the plan comes from that, not from a standard formula applied to everyone.
